# EB-5 Immigrant Investor Visa for Vietnamese and Asian Investors

*Reviewed by David Enterline, Esq., Managing Partner. Last updated: **[DATE]**.*

The EB-5 program gives investors and their families a path to U.S. permanent residence through an investment in a U.S. commercial enterprise. Investors put in either **US$800,000** (a targeted employment area project) or **US$1,050,000** (a standard project), and the investment must create at least **10 full-time jobs** for qualifying U.S. workers. Enterline & Partners is a firm of licensed U.S. immigration attorneys with offices in Ho Chi Minh City and Manila. We prepare and file the petitions and guide you through the visa process.

## EB-5 at a glance

## Benefits of the EB-5 investor visa
- No day-to-day management requirement
- No requirement for business or management experience
- No minimum education or English-language requirement
- Live, work, study or retire anywhere in the United States
- Your spouse and eligible children can receive permanent residence with you
- After permanent residence you may sponsor certain relatives, and you may qualify for U.S. citizenship after five years as a permanent resident **[attorney: confirm how conditional residence time counts]**

## The management requirement, explained
EB-5 requires that the investor be involved in the management of the business, but not that the investor run it daily. Being a limited partner in a limited partnership or a member of a limited liability company satisfies this requirement, so many investors qualify without management experience.

## Source of funds: the part that decides most cases
The most important step is showing that the money you invest was obtained lawfully, and tracing how it moves from its origin to the project. This is the hardest part of most EB-5 cases. We start by reviewing your assets and deciding which can be used as investment capital, then build the evidence file for your petition.

## Direct investment or regional center?

| | Direct investment (non-regional center) | Regional center |
|---|---|---|
| How it works | You invest in and manage your own new commercial enterprise | You invest in a project sponsored by a regional center approved by USCIS |
| Your role | Active: you are involved in operating the business | More passive: the regional center and project sponsor run the operations |
| Jobs | You must create at least 10 direct full-time jobs | Jobs may include indirect and induced jobs, under the rules in force **[attorney: confirm under the RIA]** |
| Typical projects | Any lawful business you choose | Larger developments, such as real estate or infrastructure |
| Main trade-off | Flexibility and control, with more work and responsibility | Less day-to-day work, with more reliance on the sponsor |

## Risks to understand before you invest
- Your capital must be genuinely at risk. It can lose value, and return of capital is not assured.
- Project and regional center quality varies. We recommend independent due diligence on the project's finances, the sponsor's history and the offering documents.
- Rules, processing times and program status can change. **[Attorney: add one sentence on the current regional center program status and its authorization date.]**
- An approval is not certain. Poorly documented source of funds is a common cause of delay and denial.
- A petition for conditional residence does not guarantee that conditions will be removed later; you must show the investment was sustained and the jobs requirement met.
